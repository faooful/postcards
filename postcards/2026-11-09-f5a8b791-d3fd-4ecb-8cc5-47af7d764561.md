The avalanche came down at noon and buried the road to the ribbon fair, so the village measured the drift with knitting needles and voted to move the fair uphill. They strung the pennants between two pines, kept a kettle going for anyone who arrived with wet boots, and let the oldest woman decide which ribbon won. Nobody mentioned the buried road until dark. By then the fair had its own weather: warm, loud, and slightly sugared.
