At dusk the hedgehogs of Brambleford climb the old rope bridge, each with a tiny brass tuning fork. Every plank carries a bell, and every bell must ring true before the night carts cross. Tonight four crews worked at once, and nobody rang a neighbour's bell by mistake, because each crew whistled in its own key. One bell sulked and went quiet halfway through; they wrapped it in a mitten and tried again. By moonrise the bridge sang a clean chord from one bank to the other, and the carts rolled over humming.
