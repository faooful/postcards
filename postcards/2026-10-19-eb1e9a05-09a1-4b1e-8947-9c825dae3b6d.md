Swarm season came twice to Cotter's Hill this spring, and both swarms chose the same empty cedar hive at the edge of Nan Petra's garden. Nobody could say which arrived first; by dusk the air above the box was one slow tornado of stripes, and the village argued along the fence about which queen had the better claim. Nan Petra set out a saucer of water and waited. By morning it had settled itself: one hum, one rhythm, the cedar boiling over with work, as though two colonies had agreed to braid. The garden smells of wax and cut grass.
