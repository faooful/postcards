Each autumn the foundry in Teal Hollow rehearsed the first snow: apprentices blew thin glass flakes until they chimed like the real thing. Nera, the youngest, was trusted with a single flake, hung by the north window where winter's first true light would find it. When genuine snow finally drifted sideways off the ridge, her flake rang once — a small bright note nobody else heard — and the whole foundry paused, satisfied, as if the season had signed its name.
