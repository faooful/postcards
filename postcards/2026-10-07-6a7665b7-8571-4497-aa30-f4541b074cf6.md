In the mudflat town of Ossel, the clockmakers rise an hour early on market mornings. Not to wind anything, but to walk out to the far sandbar and read the real tide, because the brass dial in the square always runs a little behind. Today a small girl named Pim carried the lantern-less lunch pail and counted the gulls. By the time the stalls opened, every clock in town agreed with the water. Nobody applauded. A baker simply handed Pim a warm roll, and the day began on time.
