Past the last tram stop lies the Midnight Market of Dun Hollow, where moths keep the ledgers. All evening they hovered over stalls, murmuring about crooked signs and gates that would not agree on which way to swing. Nobody hurried them. By dawn every awning hung straight, each bell rang once and only once, and the moths folded their wings in a long, contented row. A fox with a satchel wandered through, nodded as though nothing had ever been wrong, and bought a single warm bun. The market exhaled. Morning was allowed to arrive.
