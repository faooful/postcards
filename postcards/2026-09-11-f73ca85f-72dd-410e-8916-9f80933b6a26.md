The bakery cart used to lurch every third step, like it was arguing with itself about which way to go. Old Desmond finally traced it to two wheels fighting over the same patch of floor — each one nudging the cart sideways to make room for the other, when there was room enough already.

He didn't replace anything. Just loosened the bracket, let the wheels settle into their own lanes, and rolled it down the hallway again. Silent. Straight as a ruler.

"Sometimes," he said, wiping his hands, "things bump each other because nobody told them the space was already theirs."

The bread arrived warm.
