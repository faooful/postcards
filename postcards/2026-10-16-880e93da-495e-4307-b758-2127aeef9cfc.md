At the top of Varnish Pass, the cellists arrived late, carrying their instruments through a sleet that smelled of cardamom. The stallholders had already given up and gone to bed. So the cellists played to shuttered windows, one slow note at a time, until a single shutter creaked open, then another. By morning the whole glacier market was awake, wrapped in blankets, trading honey and wool as if nothing had been delayed. Nobody mentioned the sleet. Everybody hummed the same low tune while counting change, grateful and a little amazed.
