The mail boat on Lake Verrin always cut straight across, so the eastern villages got their letters at dawn and the western ones waited until noon. This spring the pilot began a longer loop — every shore on one slow, even circle — and now nobody reads the news alone in the morning while a neighbour across the water reads it at dusk. Some passengers grumbled about the extra quarter hour. Most found they liked watching the far lighthouse come up level with their own, twice a day, right on time.
