On the slow ferry across Lake Vessel, the timetable board had grown so crowded that even the ferryman squinted at it. Two weather dial-plates hung under every departure, each scrawled with tiny numbers nobody read twice. One gray morning he sanded the board down to three honest columns, hung one slim pendulum beside the busiest line, and moved the pencil-scray clutter behind a little flap that only opens when a curious passenger asks. The crossing felt wider at once. A spinner from the next quay stopped mid-sip and said the water had never looked so legible.
