In the hill town of Vellow, the mapmakers' guild used to carry every portrait they had ever painted, strapped to their backs in heavy oak frames. One spring a young apprentice named Oddny tried something new: she wrote down only where each portrait hung. The guild laughed, then went quiet when she crossed the ridge before lunch. By autumn the whole guild walked with folded notes in their pockets. The paintings stayed in their galleries, warm and whole, and anyone who wanted one only had to go and look.
