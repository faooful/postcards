On the salt-marsh island of Tamsin Reed, the last ferry of the night always pauses at the reeds before crossing. Passengers worried it would forget them by morning. Instead, the ferryman's daughter chalked each traveler's seat onto a slate: window or aisle, umbrella-free, humming or silent. At dawn the boat slid back, and every person found their exact bench, still faintly warm. Nobody said anything. A heron on the mooring post nodded once, which on Tamsin Reed counts as applause.
