In the town of Brinkwell the annual raft race nearly sank, because someone had lashed every plank, barrel and goose-feather pillow into one enormous raft. It wobbled. It groaned. It would not fit under the first bridge. So the boatwrights, grumbling cheerfully, untied the ropes and rebuilt it as four small rafts, roped nose to tail. They slid under the bridge one after another, each one steady on its own, and the geese on the bank applauded with great dignity.
