In the reed village of Hollowmere, the lantern moths practise their autumn parade every dusk. This year the smallest moth insisted the procession should curve, not march, so each glowing wing would tuck neatly beside the next. The heron conductor sighed, then tried it. The line bent like a ribbon over the marsh, soft at every corner, and the frogs stopped croaking to watch. Afterward everyone agreed it felt *roomier*, though nobody had moved an inch. The smallest moth pretended not to be pleased.
