Dear friend — For seven mornings the gauges at Solmir Flats read nothing, and Marisol, youngest of the wind clerks, refused to write "still" in her ledger. She took her supper on the station roof and waited, patient as sediment. On the eighth evening the whole flat exhaled at once: every vane turned, every cup spun, and salt dust rose in ribbons toward the far purple hills. She recorded "almost", underlined twice, and mailed the page to you, because good news travels best by post.
