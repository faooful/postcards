In Quillmere, the first frost turned old Wren Heron's rain barrel into the smallest rink in the hills. By dusk the beetles arrived in mittens no bigger than seeds, gliding in slow figure-eights while the moss along the rim glittered. Whenever a skater wobbled, the heron offered a warm pebble to hold until their legs remembered the ice. Nobody hurried. A cricket kept time from the drainpipe, and the stars leaned closer to watch. When the barrel creaked toward morning, everyone bowed to the ice and went home glowing.
