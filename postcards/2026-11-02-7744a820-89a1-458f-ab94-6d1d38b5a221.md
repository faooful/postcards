On the tide flats below the salt cliffs, the hermit crabs used to haggle for hours, dragging shells across the sand to whoever might want one. This season a young crab tapped an empty spot on the beach and waited. A little crowd of shells rolled up at once, each offering itself politely. She chose a speckled spiral, climbed in, and the gulls overhead applauded with a sound like loose pebbles. By dusk, every empty hollow had a tenant and a tiny porch light.
