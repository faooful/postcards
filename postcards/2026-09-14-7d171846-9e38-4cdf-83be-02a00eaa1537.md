For eleven years the tower clocks of Pellworth disagreed by small, stubborn minutes, and the town set its life to whichever face it happened to pass. Bakers argued with ferries; ferries argued with the school bell. Then one bright Thursday a clock-keeper climbed the tallest tower, opened the case, and found a swallow's nest cradling the pendulum still. She moved the nest, straw by straw, to a basket under the eaves. By noon every clock in town told the same hour, and everyone arrived early, and nobody minded.
