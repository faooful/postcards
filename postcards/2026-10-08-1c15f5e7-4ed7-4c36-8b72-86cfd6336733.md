In the hill town of Pellmoor, the weather is kept in a tall oak dresser at the inn. Each drawer holds one kind: a drizzle folded like linen, a breeze rolled into socks, a rumble of small thunder that hums when you walk past. Only one drawer opens at a time, or the drawers get jealous and the fog escapes. This afternoon I pulled out a modest sunbeam, and the whole common room went gold. The innkeeper winked and slid it shut before supper.
