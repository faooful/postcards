Every autumn, Rillmere counts its starlings. This year the birds declined to be counted until the town agreed to be counted back. So the census clerk sat all afternoon on the well's rim while forty thousand wings described her: patient, curious, fond of peppermint, slower to anger than the river. When they finally settled, they spelled a single word across the sky — *present* — and the tally, for once, felt warm. The clerk stamped the page, left it open on the sill, and let the dusk sign it too.
