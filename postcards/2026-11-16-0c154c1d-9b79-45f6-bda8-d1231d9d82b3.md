By seven in Kettlemark, the queue outside Orsa's bakery had opinions. Rye loaves came out at seven sharp, and the townsfolk had sorted themselves by instinct: the early walkers at the front, the night-shift florists at the back, and a trombonist somewhere in the middle who claimed his spot by tradition rather than arrival time. On this particular morning a light snow began falling, and someone started humming, and the whole line — quite without planning it — settled into a low chord that lasted until the door opened and the warm air rolled out over them like a second breakfast. Orsa handed loaves through the chorus and charged nobody for the harmony.
