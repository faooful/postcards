Dana drives the number seven up Olive Hill, and the old schedule made her pause at every lamp-lit corner whether or not anyone was waiting. The depot rewrote the rule this week: watch closely while the street is busy and somebody might come running; once your passengers are aboard, climb the hill and go home. On Thursday a violinist packed up by the fountain, a stranger sprinted the last block in soft shoes, and Dana swung the doors open with the whole slope glowing behind her. The tram, she wrote in the log, finally runs like breathing.
