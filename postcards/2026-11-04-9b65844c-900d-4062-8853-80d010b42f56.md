In Quillfrog Alley, where rain taps the shutters like a lazy metronome, the bookbinder woke to a complaint: the choir's sheet music lived in eleven separate pockets, and no one could find the alto line before dawn practice.

So she cut one generous folio, stitched a spine wide enough for every part, and slipped a ribbon marker where the conductor stands. By evening the singers filed in, flipped once, and gasped — every voice, one cover, room to grow.

She chalked a small note on the door: more folios on request.
