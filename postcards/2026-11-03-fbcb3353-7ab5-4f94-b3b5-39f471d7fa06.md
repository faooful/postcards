In the hill town of Wendle, the autumn parade kept stalling at the bakery corner, where a sign insisted every float turn left into a wall. The tortoises, who march slowest and notice most, simply nudged the arrow one notch right. Nobody shouted; the brass band just found itself rolling downhill toward the square, still mid-tune. By dusk the confetti had settled into the gutters like warm snow, and the sign, freshly painted, looked as if it had always known the way.
