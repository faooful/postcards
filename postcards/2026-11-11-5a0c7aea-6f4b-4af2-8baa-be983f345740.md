In the hill town of Quarrow, every chapel once rang its own bell on its own rope, and the hour arrived in seven slightly different opinions. This autumn the ringers agreed to meet in the square and pull together. The first attempt sounded like a dropped tray of spoons. The second was better. By the third evening the valley heard a single clear note roll over the rooftops, and nobody could remember what had been so hard to give up. The old ropes hang in the museum now, politely labeled.
