The theatre drifts down the canal on a raft, and tonight the stagehands have painted every backdrop the colour of late evening. A heron sits in the front row, unimpressed, until someone pulls a rope and a whole little town unfolds from the wings: tiny windows you can actually open, a bakery door that rings. The heron leans closer. By the second act it is pecking at the windows itself, delighted, and nobody has the heart to stop the show.
