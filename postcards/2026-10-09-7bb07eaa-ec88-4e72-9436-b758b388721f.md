On the far side of the Marrow Flats, a caravan of surveyors arrived at a village where every doorway used to swoop open with a flourish of trumpets and a rolling carpet. Travelers loved it for a day and grew weary by the week's end. So the villagers quietly unplugged the trumpets, rolled up the carpet, and let the doors simply be doors. Visitors stepped in, found their tea already warm, and sighed with *surprising* gratitude. Nobody missed the fanfare. The dunes outside kept their slow shimmer, and the evening arrived on time, unannounced, and entirely sufficient.
