In the hill town of Quorlby, every rooftop wore a tin weathervane, and every one of them pointed the same blank way. Then the glassblower's niece painted a tiny face on each: a rooster for the bakery, a teapot for the inn, a red-cheeked fiddle for the music hall. By supper the whole valley could read the skyline at a glance. When the wind turned stormy, the fiddle blushed brighter, and folks simply knew to bring their coats.
