In the switchyard above Bramble Hollow, the crew worked all winter on a line everyone had forgotten. By March the rails shone, and the old lever — the one that chose between the switchback route through the pines and the new straight run along the ridge — moved easily again, oiled and eager. The night inspector threw it both ways, twice, and each time the same lamps of the same small town rose up to meet the train. She logged it in pencil: both roads hold. Then she let the snowmelt have the last word.
