In the village of Lower Thimble, the bees stitch a quilt every autumn, one square per hive. This year someone noticed that one square was a shade too dark, like a puddle in a meadow. Nobody scolded it. The bees held a small council on a fencepost, hummed for a while, and then gently rethreaded it in the same soft dusk as its neighbours. By evening the quilt looked like one long, warm field. *Finally,* said the oldest bee, and took a nap in the middle of it.
