On the ridge above Starfish Cove (the starfish insisted on the credit), the hermit crab association finally agreed that twelve scattered signs along the tide pools was ten too many. At dawn they pooled their spare shells into one long bench at the pool mouth: everything a visitor might ask, lined up in a single row, biggest shell first. The old signs went into the recycling tide with quiet dignity. By evening, even the anemones could find the good pools without asking twice, and the crabs posed beside their bench, feeling considerably more streamlined.
