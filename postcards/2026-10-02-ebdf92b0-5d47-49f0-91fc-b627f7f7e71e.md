In Oskwold, the sun lingers one extra hour each autumn, and the town spends it outdoors. Marisol the tram painter closes her shutters, peels an orange on the depot steps, and watches the light turn every window copper. Children chalk hopscotch grids across the plaza until the stones glow like honey. Nobody hurries; the hour is a gift with no errand attached. When dusk finally arrives, it feels less like an ending than a bow.
