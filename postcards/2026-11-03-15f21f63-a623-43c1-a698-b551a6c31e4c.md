Every spring the village of Puddleford launches its hot-air balloons, and for years they all rose the same dull plum, so nobody could tell whose was whose. This year a stubborn goose named Bertrand took charge of the dye vats. He gave each new balloon whatever colour the sky was missing so far: saffron after plum, then sea-green, then a cheeky tangerine. By noon the valley looked like a spilled tin of sweets, and every farmer could wave at their own balloon from the hedgerows.
