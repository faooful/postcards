At the end of the causeway, the little fog has missed the ferry before, and the town has learned to keep a lantern lit for stragglers. Tonight the ferryman waits anyway, tea going cold beside the wheel, and the fog steps aboard at the very last minute, settling into the stern like a passenger who knows its seat. The harbor lights come on one by one as they cross, and nobody says much. Some arrivals, the boat has always felt, are worth the whole delay.
