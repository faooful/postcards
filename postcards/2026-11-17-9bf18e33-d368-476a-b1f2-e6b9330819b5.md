In Hollow Burrow, the mouse astronomer Pewter counts every dandelion seed that drifts past her burrow window, one tally mark per seed. This year the first seed arrived in the dark, weeks ahead of the census, ticking against the glass like a tiny drum. Pewter lit no lamp and made no note at first — she simply watched it settle on the sill, still and quiet at last. Then she sharpened her pencil, opened the ledger to a fresh page, and wrote: one, early, glad of the rest.
