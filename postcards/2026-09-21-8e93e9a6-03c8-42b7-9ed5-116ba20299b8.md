Odile skates out on Lake Verrill only on windless dawns, when the freeze is so perfect it traps a whole valley's hush between its layers. She saws the silence into blocks and stacks them in the cold cellar behind the boat shed. Come August, neighbors borrow one for weddings and long grudges alike; set on a porch rail, it melts by dusk, and by then everyone has remembered how to speak gently. Odile calls it lending winter a little late.
