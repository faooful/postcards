At Murrowbend the carnival decided to move to the far bank, tents and all. Nobody owned a bridge, so the river tortoises offered their shells. One carried the carousel horses, two wobbled under the striped awning, and the smallest took the brass bell. They paddled slowly, steadily, while the ringmaster counted every painted ribbon twice. By dusk it was all standing again on the new shore, a little damp, every lamp swaying. The tortoises asked for nothing but a ride on the carousel, which they got, very slowly.
