At the edge of the salt flats, the glassworks keeps odd hours. Every solstice the glassblowers blow one enormous bottle, and when the desert cools at dawn it hums a low, patient note across the yard. The tram depot next door has no bell of its own, so the drivers have learned to trust the hum: first tone, doors open; second, last call. This morning the note arrived early, and a hundred commuters boarded laughing, still holding unfinished toast, delighted to be wrong about the time.
