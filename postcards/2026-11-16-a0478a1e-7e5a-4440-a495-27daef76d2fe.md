Every autumn the snails of Marrowby Flats hold a census of the fog. They climb the reeds at dawn with tiny slates and tally each drifting bank by shape: the curled ones, the flat ones, the one that always looks like a sleeping goat. Nobody remembers why it matters. This year a young snail noticed three banks drifting the same way, toward the old salt road, and drew a careful arrow. The elders studied it for a very long time and then, slowly, agreed to follow.
