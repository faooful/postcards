High in the Thistlewick Pass, a crew of porcupine postmasters kept every pigeonhole shutter closed against the wind. Visitors arrived with parcels and found only blank wooden faces, and nobody knew which cubbies were busy. Then Odalie, the smallest postmaster, tied a bit of twine to each shutter so that any parcel slipping inside would tug it open. By noon the whole wall was breathing light and rustling paper. Travelers pointed and laughed with relief. Odalie only adjusted her spectacles and wrote, in tidy quills, *please do close one yourself if you'd like it quiet.*
