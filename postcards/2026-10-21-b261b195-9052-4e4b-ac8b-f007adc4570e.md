At Dunmarrow Quay, the fishwives had a rule: nobody counted the catch until the tide-gate was checked. For years the gate had been locked against a rumor, and every morning the nets came in unsorted, a single grey heap labelled *fish*. Then one drizzly dawn a girl named Odalie peeked through the keyhole and saw only an empty bucket. She said so, loudly. The gate swung open, the heap divided itself into silver, copper and striped, and the whole quay laughed until the gulls complained.
