In Collioure the net-mender Anysia rose before the gulls and found the harbor shallows silvered with sardines, three finger-widths deep, exactly where the old charts said only mullet dared to swim. The village council argued until noon: some said the current had changed its habits, others said the charts were drawn by a man who never woke early. Anysia said nothing. She mended the third tear in her net, measured the water with her oar, and wrote a new line on the chart herself — small, ink slightly smudged, but true. By evening the whole fleet had copied it, and the sardines, indifferent to all of this, kept arriving anyway.
