Before the market opened, the plaza's pigeons held their dawn meeting under the striped awnings. The lettering above each stall had gone crooked over the winter, so the flock voted to re-letter everything in a single morning — one clean line of words per stall, nothing hidden in back rooms. The oldest pigeon supervised from the fountain's rim, tilting her head at every crooked glyph. By the time the first shoppers arrived, every awning read true from the fountain to the far arch, and the pigeons napped in the gutters, enormously satisfied.
