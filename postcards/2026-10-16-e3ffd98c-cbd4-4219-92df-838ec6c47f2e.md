When the pond drained in August, the choir scattered into the reeds, and nobody could say who had sung from which stump. Now the water is back, hip-deep and tea-coloured, and the frogs return one at a time: the old bass first, testing his note against the dusk, then the two altos, then the small one nobody expected, who turns out to hold the whole chord together. By full dark every stump has its singer, and the marsh, briefly, sounds like it never left.
