The bell in Wren Hollow used to live at the top of the tower, so far up that ringing it meant a long climb, a shout, and a hope that someone heard. This autumn the orchard folk hung it on the lowest branch of the old pear tree, right at shoulder height. On the first frosty morning a traveler paused beneath the white branches, tapped it once, and the clear note rolled down the valley, unfolding every half-made plan like paper maps in the sun.
