On Copperwind Ridge, the morning drummers always arrived in a crooked row, some with a lantern-bearer ahead of them and some without, so every rhythm began a half step apart. Wren, the youngest, borrowed a long ruler of pale birch and walked the line, nudging each boot to the same chalk mark. Then she stood back and listened. The first beat landed together, clean as a dropped plate. The goats stopped chewing. Even the wind, usually late, arrived exactly on time and seemed *delighted* about it.
