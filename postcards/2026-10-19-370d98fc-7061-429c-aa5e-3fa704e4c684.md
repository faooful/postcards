The rain had washed a slick of pine needles over the switchback stair above Hollowmere, and everyone in the valley simply stopped climbing it, taking the long muddy road instead. Then the badger surveyors arrived with their chalk and their stubborn little level, tapping each step until they found the two that had shifted. They wedged them level by lantern-free dawn, and by breakfast the whole stair hummed with feet again — mail carriers, mushroom pickers, one delighted goat. Nobody thanked the badgers out loud, but somebody left tea cakes at the bottom step, still warm.
