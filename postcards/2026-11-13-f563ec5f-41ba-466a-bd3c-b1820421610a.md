In Dunmarrow, the patchwork balloon waits on the dune until the wind quits gossiping. All morning the gusts tugged at its ropes, and three times the crew had to haul it back down, sand in their boots and their sandwiches. Nobody cut a rope. They played cards, watched the flags droop, and argued kindly about clouds. At noon the air went still as a held breath, and up the balloon rose, slow and sure, with every pocket of its quilt pinned exactly where it belonged.
