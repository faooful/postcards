On the fern terraces of Lower Bramble, harvest mice keep a registry of dewdrops, shelving each morning's crop by weight and clarity. One dawn the dew arrived early — every droplet queued at the gate before the registrar had lit her lamp. The mice improvised: whisker-measurements on the fly, a courier relay along the frond stems, tiny ledgers balanced on seed-caps. By noon the collection sat in order, shinier than ever, and the registrar added a new column to the ledger titled *arrived ahead of us*. The terraces smelled of wet green and accomplished paperwork.
