At the Sparrow Exchange in Lower Puddlewick, the night shift arrived punctually at whisker-dusk: six small birds, one enormous switchboard, and a supervisor who spoke only in telegraph clicks. Their job was simple in principle — when one caller was connected, the previous caller's line stayed warm beside it, and no plug ever slipped out just because someone dialed a new number. By three in the morning the sparrows had it down to an art: cords arranged in patient rows, every conversation held open exactly as long as its caller wanted, and the board's little brass shutters rising and falling like contented eyelids. The supervisor clicked twice, which the sparrows had long ago agreed meant *pleased*. Outside, the town slept, unaware that somewhere a very small, very diligent flock was keeping everyone's lines in step.
