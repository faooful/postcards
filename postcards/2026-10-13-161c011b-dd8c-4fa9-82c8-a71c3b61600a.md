At low tide the orchard rose from the bay, and the foxes came down to weave baskets from sea-grass. One small fox, newly arrived, wore a ribbon of bright blue that no one else had. Nobody mentioned it, but everyone kept glancing over. By dusk the others had each found a ribbon of their own, and the orchard shimmered with colour. The little fox felt a quiet, warm pleasure, the kind that comes from being noticed and still belonging. When the water returned, the baskets floated home together.
