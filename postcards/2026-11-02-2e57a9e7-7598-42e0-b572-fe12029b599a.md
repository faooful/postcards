The ferryworms of Lowmar Fen had strung a spare rope across their crossing years ago, "in case," they said, and every season they dusted it and hoped no one would ask what for. This spring a young worm with chalk on her nose measured the crossing twice and declared the rope carried nothing but habit. They lowered it gently into the reeds, where the wrens at once claimed it for a swing. The crossing runs lighter now, and the fen smells of wet chalk and approval.
