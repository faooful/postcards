At Dunmere Fen the marsh orchestra refused to begin until every cricket had found its reed. Last season the conductor, an impatient heron, started when only the first fiddler chirped, and the whole fen played a ragged, hurried song. This year she stood very still on one leg. One by one the crickets settled, the frogs cleared their throats, and the dragonflies tuned their wings. Only when the last reed trembled did she nod. The music rose all at once, whole and unhurried, and the fen felt, for one evening, entirely in agreement with itself.
