By morning, every weathervane in Aldery Meadow had swung to the same unlikely north — the fox on the mill, the bicycle on the schoolhouse, the tin heron on the chapel. Hesper, who had painted them all years ago, laced her boots and followed where they pointed. The whole town trailed her through bramble and barley, arguing pleasantly about the weather, until the arrows converged on a spring nobody remembered, singing cold and patient beneath the stones. They stayed till noon, agreeing on nothing except that the day had chosen them.
