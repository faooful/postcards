Before the festival, the masons of Coppergate walked their borrowed-span bridge stone by stone, tapping each one and listening for the note it gave back. Two joints answered hollow, and the crowd groaned — but the masons grinned and chalked the spots, saying a hollow note found now is a gift. By dusk the replacements were seated and the whole span rang like a struck bell under their hammers. At first starlight the town's children ran across, and the bridge held every footstep, singing back.
