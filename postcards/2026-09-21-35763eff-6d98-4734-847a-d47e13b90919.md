October in Nethermoor arrives one blanket at a time. Odile, who keeps the last inn below the pass, spent years stacking her spare quilts in loose, hopeful towers that slumped by midnight, leaving cold stripes between the folds. This year she shook each blanket to its full measure and laid them close, edge against edge, until the shelf held every one without a sliver of waste. Late travelers, half-frozen and fumbling in the dark, found the warmth exactly where their hands reached. Odile called it nothing clever — just winter, arranged politely.
