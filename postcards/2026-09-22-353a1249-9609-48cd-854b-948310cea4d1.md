Every autumn, Odette of Gel Hollow takes down the roads nobody travels anymore. She works by lamplight with a soft eraser and a kettle of mint, and one by one the unused lines lift off the maps of the low marches. Farmers swear a vanished road still smells faintly of pepper for a week. When she finishes, she hangs the lighter maps in the window, and the wind — with nothing left to argue with — simply moves on to the next valley, whistling.
