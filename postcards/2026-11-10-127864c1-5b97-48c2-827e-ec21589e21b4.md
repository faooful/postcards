Each dawn the wind combs two caravan tracks across the salt flat, and each dawn they drift apart by a hand's width. The surveyor sets her brass pins anyway, pacing the mirror to keep both lines at one height. Travelers laugh, then stop laughing when the horizon finally holds still. She chalks the second track, steps back, and lets the morning arrive evenly: two clean furrows, one flat light, the whole plain briefly certain of itself.
