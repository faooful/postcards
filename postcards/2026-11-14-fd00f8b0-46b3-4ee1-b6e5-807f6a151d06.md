Every October a low hum settles over Tansy Ford, and nobody agrees where it starts. Marisol, who tunes pianos and trusts no secondhand note, went out with a paper cone and infinite patience. She walked past the bakery, the drying racks, the arguing ducks, pausing at each doorway like a guest counting coats. At the old mill she stopped: the wheel, slightly off its axle, droning on faithfully. She wrote it down, folded the map into her sleeve, and hummed the whole way home, finally in tune.
