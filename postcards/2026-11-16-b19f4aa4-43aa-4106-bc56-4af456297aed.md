The snail race at Brackenwhistle used to tie every racer to its grandmother with a ribbon, so whole families crawled in tangled clumps and nobody could tell who was winning. This year the marshal snipped every ribbon at dawn. Each snail got a lane of its own, chalked straight across the mossy green. The grandmothers did not mind at all; they settled on a warm stone to cheer. By noon the course was tidy and quiet, and every small shell gleamed in the drizzle.
