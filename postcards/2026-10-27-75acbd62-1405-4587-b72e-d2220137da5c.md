In the village of Brindleshaw, the snails had always kept their spare shells stacked in the potting shed, each one a slightly different size. This autumn the gardener built them a single long annex along the greenhouse wall, with one sliding glass pane that opens as wide as anyone needs. Two families moved in first, a little nervous, carrying their mossy rugs. By evening they were pressing their antennae to the glass, watching rain bead and wander, delighted that nothing had to be measured twice.
