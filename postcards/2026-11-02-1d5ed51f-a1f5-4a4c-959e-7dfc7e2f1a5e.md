In the hill village of Pellow Rise, every household once kept its own well: one for soup water, one for washing, one for wishes. Children forgot which bucket went where, and the wishes kept tasting of onions. This autumn the masons dug a single deep shaft in the square and roofed it with green slate. Now everyone lowers the same rope. The soup is clearer, the laundry smells of rain, and the wishes, people swear, come back up a little warmer than they went down.
