The beetles of Saltwhistle Flats hold a tea contest every autumn, and this year the copper kettle refused to sing. Old Marrow tapped it, turned it, and listened to the hiss for most of an afternoon. Then the youngest beetle spotted a pebble wedged in the spout, small and smug. Out it popped. The kettle whistled so loudly that a passing tumbleweed paused to listen. Everyone agreed the cardamom tasted brighter for the wait.
