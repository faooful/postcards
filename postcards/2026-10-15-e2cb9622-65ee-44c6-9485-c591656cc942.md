On the windy cliffs of Gullsby, a small tea cart sits folded in the corner of every ledge, no bigger than a mitten. Whistle twice and it unfolds: a kettle, a striped awning, a puffin in an apron asking what you need. If you like the company, you can bolt the cart to the rock and it stays put all afternoon while the gulls shuffle over to make room. Today the fog rolled in sideways, and nobody spilled a drop.
