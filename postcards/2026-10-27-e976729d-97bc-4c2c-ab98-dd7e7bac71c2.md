In the marsh town of Little Wending, every heron used to bring its own bench to the reading green. Some creaked, some wobbled, and one leaned so far that its owner read sideways. This autumn they finally agreed to share one long bench of pale ash. There was a lot of shuffling and polite wing-tucking at first. By dusk they were settled, each with a little room to stretch, all watching the reeds turn copper together.
