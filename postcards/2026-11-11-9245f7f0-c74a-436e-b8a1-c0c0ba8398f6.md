At perihelion, the comet split its tail into two ribbons, and for a while neither knew which way the sun preferred. One curled toward the cold, one toward the light. They braided instead of choosing, trading dust and a little static. Astronomers below set down their coffee and argued about what it meant. By morning the two ribbons had woven a single bright thread across the dark, and the sky looked, for once, tidily stitched. Nobody had to say which tail was right.
