At dawn the glassblowers of Marrow Canyon shouted four names into the rock and waited. Each echo came back clearer than it left, rounded by the cliffs like a pebble in a stream. Someone hung a small brass bell where the sound turned, so travelers could hear how much of the canyon remained. A goat wandered through, nodded gravely at the bell, and ate a paper map. Nobody minded. By noon every apprentice was humming the same four notes, and the whole canyon felt pleasantly, quietly finished.
