In the hill village of Pemberwick, the goats took over ladder duty this autumn. Each morning they line the ladders up beside the pear rows, the most recently climbed one always nearest the gate. When a picker sets one down, the goat beside it nudges the next ladder over without being asked. A ladder left in the hedge is remembered and wheeled back exactly where it stood. Nobody taught them this. The pickers swear the harvest feels lighter, and the goats accept payment only in windfalls.
