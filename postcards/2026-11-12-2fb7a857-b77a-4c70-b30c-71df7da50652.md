The wind set Odile's balloon down on the salt flats with three hours of daylight left and not a road in sight. She walked toward the only shadow on the horizon, a dry well with a shepherd asleep against its rim.

"Dropped out of the sky, did you?" he said, and split his flatbread without waiting for an answer. By the time the coals settled, she had helped him whistle in a lost lamb, and he had helped her stitch the balloon's torn seam with gut thread.

At dawn she rose on the first breeze. The lamb watched until the balloon was a dot, then went back to sleep.
