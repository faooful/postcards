For sixty years, Harrowfen's two towers rang on the quarter hour, always a half-second apart, so every chime arrived bruised. Nobody remembered why the bell-ringers quarreled; they only knew the town set its mood by the smudge of sound. Then one windless evening a new ringer climbed the west tower, heard the echo stumble down the lane, and simply waited — one breath — before her stroke. The east tower, startled, matched her. By midnight the whole valley was listening to the two of them ring, for the first time, together.
