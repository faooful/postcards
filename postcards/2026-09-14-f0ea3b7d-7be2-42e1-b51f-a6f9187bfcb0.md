The mail boat had lost a week to fog, so Ines took the balloon instead. She rose above the atoll with the week's letters tucked in a canvas satchel, and for an hour the islands lay below her like a hand of scattered cards. When the wind shifted kindly, she dropped each bundle into its own lagoon of lamplight — plink, plink, plink — and watched the shuttered windows open one after another. By the time she landed, half the archipelago was awake and reading, and someone had left soup on her doorstep, still warm.
