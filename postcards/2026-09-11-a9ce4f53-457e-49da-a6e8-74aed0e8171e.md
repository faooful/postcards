Out past the reeds, three kite menders set up their folding table every dawn, patching torn sails with whatever scraps the wind had left behind. Today a stranger's kite drifted down still holding its old, awkward shape, tangled and stiff in the crosswind. The menders traded glances, then reached for the pattern they already trusted — the one that had flown true on a dozen other kites all summer. They cut, stitched, and matched the seams so closely you'd think it had always been that way. When they let it go again, it caught the same gust as its neighbors and rose without a wobble, three shapes now moving as one across the pale morning sky.
