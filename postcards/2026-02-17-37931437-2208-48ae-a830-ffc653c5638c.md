Every February the ferry crews of Cutter Marsh trade their horns for steam whistles, one note per boat, and the whole delta answers in a ragged chord that wakes the otters and rattles Mrs. Ferrow's greenhouse glass. She swears the low note belongs to her late husband's tug, still running its route somewhere out past the reed banks. The crews don't correct her. Some evenings, if you stand on the south jetty with your boots in the cold mud, you can almost pick out which whistle is which — and one of them, honestly, sounds pleased with itself.
