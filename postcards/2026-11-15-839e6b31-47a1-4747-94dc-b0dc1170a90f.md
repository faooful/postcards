In the hill village of Pellow, the beekeepers noticed their hives had grown so heavy the shelves bowed. Every bee, it turned out, had been carrying home a full portrait of the queen each time a single petal changed color. The keepers sat in the clover all afternoon, listening, counting hums, until the pattern came clear. By dusk they had a plan scratched on a slate: lighter baskets, fewer portraits, one quiet bell instead of a hundred. The bees seemed relieved.
