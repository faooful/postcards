In Bransk the twin canals never spoke. The east kept its own tide tables; the west kept a looser, saltier schedule, and the ferrymen argued charts at every crossing. One stormy night rearranged the sandbars, and by morning the two waters had met — a single current, one set of ripples, still bickering in small eddies out of habit. The ferrymen celebrated by racing nothing at all, downstream, together, at last in the same direction.
