At the edge of the red valley, the weavers wait for the monsoon to lean against their eaves. When it does, they coax it into the looms and fold it, seam by seam, into carpets that smell faintly of rain. Nobody counts the storms; they only agree that each year's pattern must not grow looser at the corners. A child holds the shuttle and learns the rhythm from the weather instead of the song. By dusk the whole street is warm, and the river has a new, quieter color.
