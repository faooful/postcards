Nobody in Wrenbury had planned a regatta. The gale simply untied every line on Carrow Lane at once, and by midmorning the washing was airborne — sheets, tablecloths, one stubborn pillowcase — drifting downstream in loose, cheerful formation. Milo ran the whole length of the towpath with a broom for a mast. By dusk the neighbors had lashed the lot to a raft and were arguing, happily, over who steered. The river won. The pillowcase, having led the fleet, refused to come home.
