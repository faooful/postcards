In the river town of Ostrava Bend, the dawn market used to open wherever the previous evening's crowd had wandered off to: a bakery stall one day, a rope-maker's yard the next. Ines the weaver found this charming for about a week. Then she hung a bright red ribbon over the old stone bridge and declared that every morning would begin there. Grumbling followed, mostly from the geese. By Thursday, everyone arrived humming, knowing exactly where the warm bread would be. Anyone who still *liked* wandering could simply say so.
