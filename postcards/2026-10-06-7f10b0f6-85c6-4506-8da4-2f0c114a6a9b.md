Up on the ridge, the observatory's apprentice developed two plates of the same dawn. The first had grabbed at the sky and crowded it; the second let the horizon stay exactly where it was, quiet and unhurried. She pinned them side by side on the corkboard and stepped back until the frames blurred together. The night assistant drifted past, nodded once, and turned the dome toward evening. Outside, a jawbone-dry wind combed the grama grass while the plates cooled, each star settling into the corner of the frame it had chosen all along.
