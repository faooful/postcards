At the cliffside town of Belhollow, the stonemasons spent a week lifting every terrace step onto one long, level rail. Travelers had grown used to hopping the old offsets; now the path simply held them. The masons measured twice, left the flowerpots where they stood, and swept the last grit away before dusk. By morning the goats no longer paused, the postcart rolled straight to the well, and nobody could remember which step had once been the difficult one.
