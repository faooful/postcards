The mail tortoise arrived at Quillon Flats three days late, which everyone agreed was exactly on time. Strapped to her shell was a satchel of dyed ribbons: saffron, moss, plum, a teal like shallow creek water. Each household stepped out, turned a ribbon over in their fingers, and chose one for the front gate. By dusk the whole dry-wash street had become a row of small, bright decisions, and the tortoise, pleased with herself, ate a single lettuce leaf and began the long walk to the next village.
