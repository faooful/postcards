Nobody in Quillmere agreed on who walked first in the Sunday parade, so the milliners settled it the only way they knew: by hat. Tallest brim led, then widest, then whoever had sewn the most stubborn feather. The sign painter went sixth because her hat was sensible, and she pretended not to mind. By autumn the order had shifted twice and settled into something nobody could justify and everybody liked — a procession that began with a shout and ended with the smallest hat in town, held very high by a very small person.
