At Quenby Rock the tide pulls back each evening, and the crabs gather to rehearse. Their first attempts are all clatter. A small one named Pim keeps counting the beats out loud, wrong every time, and every time starting over with a cheerful click. By the darkest hour the clatter has found a rhythm, a rolling little tune that sets the anemones swaying. Nobody applauds, because nobody has hands to spare. But the water comes home carrying the sound, and the whole shoreline hums along, delighted, until morning.
