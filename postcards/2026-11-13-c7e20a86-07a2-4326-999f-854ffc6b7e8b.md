Every October, Bruna the badger unboxed the meadow's lost-and-found for hikers — forty-one mittens, sorted by warmth. This year every mitten wore a tag: names, cabins, promises of cinnamon bread. Nothing needed finding anymore. So she hauled the box to the highest pasture and, under a sky the color of cold water, hung each mitten from the juniper branches like small red flags. By dusk the wind had claimed them all as its own, and the meadow, for once, was perfectly accounted for.
