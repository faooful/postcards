The ferry at Inkwhistle Crossing is folded, twice daily, from a single sheet of paper, and everyone knows paper hates water. So the town's two harbormasters spent a decade arguing in chalk on the pier wall: one swore by the morning tide, the other by the evening's. Last spring a visiting accountant simply wrote both tables side by side, drew one calm line where they agreed, and left. The ferry, they say, has crossed on schedule ever since — and the chalk wars ended not with surrender but with two hands agreeing to hold the same rope. The mist on the water this morning looked pleased about it.
