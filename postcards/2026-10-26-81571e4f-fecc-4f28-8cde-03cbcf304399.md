In the hill town of Osterbrae the singers had always crowded into a cramped loft above the congregation, peering over a railing to find their notes. This autumn someone suggested they simply stand beside everyone instead. So they carried the music stands down the creaking stairs, one careful flight at a time, and set them along the east wall where the afternoon light pools. Nobody sang any differently. Somehow, though, every voice could finally be heard, and the bell-ringer swears the pigeons hummed along.
