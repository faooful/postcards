In the reed country of Pellowmere, the otters woke to find their favorite stream simply gone. Not dried, not dammed: it had never been dug on this side of the hill. They swam the old channels anyway, asking frogs and herons, until a mole explained, kindly, that the water belonged to a spring nobody had uncovered yet. So the otters stopped pouring buckets into an empty ditch. They drew a map, marked the spring with a pebble, and waited, whiskers twitching, for someone with a shovel.
