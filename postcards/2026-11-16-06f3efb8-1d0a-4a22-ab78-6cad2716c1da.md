In the hill town of Tumbleshire, the hedgehogs hold a wool market every frost. This year a young one named Pell gathered scraps from every stall: a striped corner here, a mustard square there, half a tassel nobody wanted. The other hedgehogs laughed until Pell laid the pieces out on the cobbles and stitched them, edge to ragged edge, into a quilt that somehow showed the whole valley. By dusk the whole market had wandered over to look, and someone asked where the blue river patch came from. Pell pointed, delighted, right back to the stall.
