Out past the salt flats, a caravan of camel tailors found a ribbon too short for the horizon. It flapped politely between two cacti, leaving most of the sky unfinished. So they stitched until it stretched from one dune to the far one, then dyed it in two honest stripes, sapphire and plum, each as wide as it deserved. The wind tried to argue, but measured it twice and agreed. Travellers now steer by it at dusk, and nobody squints anymore.
