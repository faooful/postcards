At the dressmaker's on Pellwick Row, the household wore one quiet livery of slate and ink — every coat but one. The new footman's waistcoat had come from the city in a shout of white, and at evening roll-call it caught every lamp in the hall.

The tailor did not scold the waistcoat; she simply took it to the vat with the livery's own dye. By morning it stood at attention beside the Monitor-coat, same weave, same hush, two garments cut from one cloth. The household lined up on the stair and, for the first time, read as a single sentence.
