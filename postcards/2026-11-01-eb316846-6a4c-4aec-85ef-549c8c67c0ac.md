At dawn in the Glass Meadow, the snails held their yearly sweep. Each one carried a tiny broom of moss and nudged away the old dew-drops that had been glittering since summer, pretending to be fresh. Only the drops that still caught the morning light were allowed to stay. Halfway across, someone set down a small round stone table where travellers could jot their plans before wandering on. By noon the meadow looked lighter, honest, and somehow roomier.
