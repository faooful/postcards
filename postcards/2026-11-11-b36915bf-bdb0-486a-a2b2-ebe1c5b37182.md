Low tide gave the rock-pool orchestra one narrow ledge, and the hermit crabs kept spilling onto a second, soggier row. A patient starfish spent the afternoon sliding shells an inch here, an inch there, folding the cymbals flat and tucking the kelp harp sideways. By sunset every player fit on the dry ledge, elbow to claw, and nobody had touched the barnacles' beloved velvet seats. The first note rang out clean across the water, and a passing gull stopped mid-squawk to listen.
