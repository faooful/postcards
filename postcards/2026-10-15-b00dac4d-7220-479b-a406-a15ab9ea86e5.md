On market morning in the river town of Pell, three herons arrived with string and chalk. They didn't sell anything. They only walked the square, pacing out where each awning should stand, so the cheese seller's canopy stopped bumping the weaver's. By noon every stall sat in a tidy row, each with its sign at the left and its till at the right. When a barge tied up and the square grew narrow, the stalls folded inward like paper fans. Nobody had to shout. The herons left before lunch, quite pleased.
