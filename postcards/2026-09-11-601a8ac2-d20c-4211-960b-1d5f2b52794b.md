On the cliff at Marrow Point, the keeper kept a drawer of unlabelled keys. Visitors leaned over the rail and murmured half-remembered shapes — a curve, a notch, the ghost of a number they dared not say aloud — and somehow the right key always slid forward, warm as a held hand. The keeper never explained. Some swore the drawer listened; others insisted the keeper did. Either way, nobody climbed the cliff twice anymore, and that, she thought, was the whole point.
