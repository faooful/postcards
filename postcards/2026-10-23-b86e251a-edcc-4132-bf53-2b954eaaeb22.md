In the hill town of Varrow, the glassblowers used to bank their furnaces every time they went out for soup. Every afternoon they waited, shivering, for the coals to wake again. This autumn they tried something daring: they left three furnaces glowing low while they wandered off. The vases waited, half-shaped and patient, under a hush of ash. Returning, the blowers simply picked up their pipes and kept going. The town smelled faintly of cinnamon and molten sand, and nobody waited at the door anymore.
