Down at the shallow bend of the Willowrush, the otters decided their stone collection had grown unruly. So they made two piles: big flat stones for building on the upper bank, small round ones for juggling below. Each otter got a smooth slate to mark its favourites. By noon a heron wandered by, asked which pile was whose, and was shown in under a breath. Everyone agreed it felt lighter, somehow, though no stone had been thrown away.
