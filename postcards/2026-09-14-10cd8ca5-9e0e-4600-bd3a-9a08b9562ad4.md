On the overnight line through the Vellagio hills, conductor Odile decided everyone should wake facing the light. Each night at the mountain halt she would shuffle the sleeper cars — a quiet coup between the last owl and the first bird — until the whole train ran east-facing by dawn. Passengers began comparing sunrises over breakfast, each swearing theirs arrived first. Odile poured the coffee, kept her secret, and let the morning take the credit.
