The signal clerk at Herringlight Quay used to swap every moored boat whenever a flag she couldn't read fluttered at the harbor mouth. Two wrecks taught her the new rules: a boat already flying the quay's own colors stays put no matter what the far-off flags say, and once a traveler has repainted a hull in their own name, her morning touch-ups leave that paint alone. The harbor master's log for the day reads simply: flags confirmed, boats kept, paints preserved.
