In the hill village of Brindlecombe, three ferrets took over the bakery's crooked top shelf after closing. They had no tools, only whiskers and an opinion. The jars of seeds went into one snug line, the rolling pins learned to lie side by side, and the bell jar of spare buttons moved to the far corner where it could see everything. By morning the baker found a shelf that fit in a single glance, and a small floury pawprint on the latch, as if signing the work.
