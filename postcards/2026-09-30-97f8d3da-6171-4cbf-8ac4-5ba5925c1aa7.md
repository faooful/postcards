In Stonemeet, the harbor seals sing at first light, and nobody knows why. Osla, who hears almost nothing, feels each note arrive through the pier boards before the water shows it. One gray morning the foghorn answers wrong—or so the fishermen say—until Osla presses her palm to the planks and finds the seals have learned its note and are harmonizing, three voices under one low hum. Now the whole town arrives early, boots off, hands flat on the wood, listening the way she taught them.
