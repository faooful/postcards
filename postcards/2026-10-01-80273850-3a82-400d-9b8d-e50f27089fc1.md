In Upper Bracken, the observatory is not a building but an agreement: every ten years the dome moves to wherever the night is darkest. This year the lot fell to us. We greased the log rollers at dawn, argued politely about the route past the mill, and by dusk the whole village walked beside the dome like a slow parade. Old Nesbit swore the stars looked nervous. When we let it settle on Widow Crag, someone unlatched the shutter, and the sky poured in as though it had been waiting on us the whole way.
