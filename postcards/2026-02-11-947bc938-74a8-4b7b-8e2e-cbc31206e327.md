Two rivers met under the bridge at Vell, and for a day their masons argued about which one had moved. The north river had rerouted itself in the night — its ferry landing, its steps, everything — and every cart that crossed at dawn found the road ending in air. The carts blamed the bridge; the bridge blamed the carts; the ferryman shrugged and poled on.

A boy who fished under the arch settled it. He had watched the north river move and could name the very bend it had abandoned. The road needed one stone moved to meet the new bank — the masons laid it before noon, and by evening the carts rolled through as if nothing had ever wandered.

The bridge kept a small sign after that: *when the river moves, ask the fisherman, not the carts.*
