The balloon post left at dawn, as it always did, even the morning the east wind arrived with its arguments. Wren Ballantyne checked her ropes anyway and let the baskets of mail swing up into a sky the color of weak tea. Somewhere past the second volcano, she knew, the islands were waiting — farmers, a schoolteacher, one very patient poet. The wind could sulk all it wanted. The letters were warm, the day was long, and she had deliveries to make, one island after the next.
