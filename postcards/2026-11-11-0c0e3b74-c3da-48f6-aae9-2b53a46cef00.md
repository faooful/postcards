Twice a year the twin peaks above Auchterlen swap their smoke: the western one exhales grey, the eastern one white, until the wind forgets which is which. This season the village's swallows arrived early and circled, uncertain, reading the columns like a page turned upside down. Old Mirren climbed to the saddle with a fistful of chalk and marked the ledge, so the birds could tell the cones apart. By dusk every swallow had found its roost, and the peaks went on pretending nothing had happened.
