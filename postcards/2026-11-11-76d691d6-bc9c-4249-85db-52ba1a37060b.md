At the marsh crossing, the herons took over the cargo lists. Every crate got a row and every row got a column: weight, colour, whether it hummed. The ducks complained that their crates were too wide and spilled into the next column, so the herons stretched the margins and the lines settled. By dusk a sleepy otter could tap down the list with one claw, open any crate, and find exactly what it promised. The tide came in, politely, in single file.
