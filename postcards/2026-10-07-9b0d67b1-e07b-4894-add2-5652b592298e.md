On the terraced hills above Varrow, the beekeepers woke to find the newest hives wandering in circles, unsure which meadows were worth the flight. So the old keepers climbed up with buckets of white chalk and drew two bold arrows on the slope, one toward the clover and one toward the thyme. By noon the young bees were humming straight along the lines, and the hill smelled faintly of honey and *relief*. Nobody erased the arrows. Rain will do that eventually, if the bees ever decide they no longer need them.
