A storm came through Millrace Falls around midnight and tangled every line in the telephone exchange, all six hundred of them, into one enormous knot. Odette, the night operator, made tea and started pulling threads. Caller by caller the switchboard lit up again: the bakery wanting flour, twins arguing about a dog, a fisherman reading weather aloud to nobody. By dawn the last jack clicked home, and for one suspended moment the whole town seemed to be talking at once, warm and overlapping, before Odette finally let herself yawn.
