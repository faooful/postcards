Mira ran the smallest stall at the Tuesday market, three baskets wide, and every time a gust came through she'd watch her neighbors' tables buckle — jars sliding, labels turned unreadable in the scramble.

She'd built hers differently. When the wind pressed in, the baskets eased back, shoulder to shoulder, labels shortening to their boldest letters, but always staying in one tidy line.

A boy asked why her stall never looked crowded in the narrow gap by the flower cart.

"Everything just needs to know how to give a little," she said, "without giving up its place in line."
