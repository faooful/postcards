In Anselm's high valley the avalanche bell hangs from the iron arch above the post road. Every winter it rings when the drift leans too far, and for years the villagers ran before the second chime finished. This October, Marisol, who oils the hinge each season, noticed the drift leaning a finger's width further than the bell allowed. She slid the striker half a breath lower, and the whole valley finally slept until dawn. Courage, it turns out, can be a small adjustment made before anyone knows it was needed.
