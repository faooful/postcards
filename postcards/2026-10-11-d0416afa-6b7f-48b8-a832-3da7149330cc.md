On Thursdays the moss-green train rolled into Pebble Hollow an hour late, and nobody minded, because it carried the quilt. Every station stitched on a patch, and the quilt had grown so long the conductor wore it as a scarf through three carriages. This week the last patch was stubborn, a square of sky-blue that refused every thread. Passengers took turns coaxing it. At dusk it finally lay flat, and the whole platform sighed like a kettle going quiet. Then they unrolled it for the stars.
