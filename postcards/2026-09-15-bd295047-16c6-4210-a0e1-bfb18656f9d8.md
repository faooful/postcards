Mira had covered for three leads that season, and each time the stage manager handed her the same folder: not a script, just blocking notes — where to stand, when to turn, which door to exit through so the light caught her face instead of her shoulder.

Tonight the actual lead sat third row in a coat, watching. Mira wasn't performing. She was walking the empty stage before the house opened, testing whether the chalk mark by the door lined up with where the spotlight would land, and whether an entrance nudged a half-second earlier now let the next one breathe. It did. She erased the old mark, drew the new one cleaner, and switched off the ghost light on her way out.
