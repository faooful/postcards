Every autumn the glasshouse at Ferroweld kept its fogged panes curtained off, and visitors assumed the garden beyond had simply ended. Then a young horticulturist named Pell slid one panel aside and discovered the winter roses still blooming, blurred silver behind the frost. Instead of clearing the whole wall, she oiled a single hinge and let the pane lean open, so the roses showed through while the warm room kept its shape. Travelers now gather at that corner to watch green light wobble past their reflections, delighted that nothing had to be moved — only made see-through.
