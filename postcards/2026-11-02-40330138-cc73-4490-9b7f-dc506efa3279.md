On the rooftops of Brindlecombe, every weathervane once pointed somewhere different, and the pigeons had given up on directions entirely. One blustery evening a small crow named Ottoline climbed from roof to roof with a pocketful of oil and patience. She coaxed the stubborn copper rooster, soothed the nervous tin fish, and gently nudged the brass ship. By midnight they all leaned the same way, humming softly, and the pigeons finally flew home in a tidy, grateful line.
