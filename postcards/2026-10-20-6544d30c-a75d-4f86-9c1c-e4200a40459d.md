In the salt-marsh town of Pennywick, the cartographers drew a map that refused to fit its frame. One side wanted the road centered, the other wanted the ferry dock visible. Everyone argued kindly for an afternoon. Then a surveyor's apprentice suggested *two* maps: a wide one that centered everything, and a pocket one that kept the dock in view. Both were pinned up before supper. Travelers consulted whichever suited their pockets, and nobody ever missed the ferry again. The apprentice went home humming, deeply relieved.
