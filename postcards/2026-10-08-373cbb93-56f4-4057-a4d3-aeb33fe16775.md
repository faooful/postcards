Down on the reed delta, the river otters have opened a ferry office in an old upturned canoe. The first window never closes: that's where the familiar crossings are sold, same as always, to beavers carrying willow and herons in a hurry. But on the far end someone has nailed up a little bell, and every time a traveller rings it, the otters paddle off and dredge a brand-new channel through the marsh. Nobody knows where the new channels go yet. The herons are thrilled about it.
