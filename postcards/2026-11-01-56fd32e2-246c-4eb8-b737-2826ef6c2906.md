The weaver ants of Quillon Ridge were halfway across the gorge when the wind rose, so they simply held on. Each ant gripped the next, and the living thread swayed over the drop like a patient question mark. Below, a beetle asked why they didn't hurry. "The far ledge isn't going anywhere," said the lead ant, "and neither are we." By dusk the bridge was finished, humming faintly, and the first leaf-laden traveler tiptoed across, delighted to find the morning's waiting had been worth every knot.
