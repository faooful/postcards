The funicular city of Veldbran ran on a chalkboard of slots: one row per tram car, drawn in square-handed chalk, holding each car's place for a generation. When the hill's signal cable washed out one gray Tuesday, Mara in the dispatch tower neither panicked nor erased. Rain smudged the top of the board unreadable, but the slots below held. New cars that arrived in the fog were chalked into empty slots on their lines; nothing was rubbed out, because nobody could prove a car had truly left the hill. Only when the cable crew's lamp flashed three times — the all-clear, every station accounted for — did Mara strike through the cars the fog had carried away. By morning the board was clean, the order held, and the oldest cars sat exactly where the first chalk had put them.
