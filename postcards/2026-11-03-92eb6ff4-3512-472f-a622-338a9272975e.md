Dear friend,

In Sallowmere the zebra crossings have taken to walking. Each dawn the stripes shuffle down the lane and repaint themselves wherever the foot traffic decides to gather, and the crossing-painter — a stooped woman with a bucket of white and a cudgel of patience — follows behind, blessing each new stripe so the town will honor it.

Today the stripes settled into a tidy ring around the well, one stripe for every path that meets there, and the painter stood a long while deciding which path was the wide one. She picked it, marked it twice, and let the rest keep their lists. By dusk every walker in Sallowmere crossed exactly where the paint said, and nobody argued, because the paint was set before the first shoe fell.

The moon came up ordinary and dutiful. Soup was had. — Yours from the flattest town in the fen
